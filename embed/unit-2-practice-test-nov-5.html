Rationalize the denominator

In plain words

  • Your final answer can't have a square root or an \(i\) on the bottom.
  • Get rid of it by multiplying top and bottom by the same thing. That's the same as multiplying by 1, so the value doesn't change.
  • One root on the bottom, like \(\sqrt3\): multiply by \(\tfrac{\sqrt3}{\sqrt3}\), because \(\sqrt3\cdot\sqrt3=3\).
  • Two terms on the bottom, like \(2+\sqrt3\) or \(1-i\): flip the middle sign (the conjugate) and multiply by that over itself.
  • Why it works: \((a+b)(a-b)=a^2-b^2\), so the root or \(i\) gets squared away. Remember \(i^2=-1\).
R1
Rationalize: \(\dfrac{6}{\sqrt3}\)
\[\begin{aligned} \text{Times }\tfrac{\sqrt3}{\sqrt3}:\quad & \frac{6\sqrt3}{\sqrt3\cdot\sqrt3}=\frac{6\sqrt3}{3}\\[8pt] \text{Simplify:}\quad & 2\sqrt3 \end{aligned}\]
Answer
\(2\sqrt3\)
R2
Rationalize: \(\dfrac{5}{2+\sqrt3}\)
\[\begin{aligned} \text{Conjugate:}\quad & 2+\sqrt3\ \to\ 2-\sqrt3\\[8pt] \text{Times }\tfrac{2-\sqrt3}{2-\sqrt3}:\quad & \frac{5(2-\sqrt3)}{(2+\sqrt3)(2-\sqrt3)}\\[8pt] \text{Bottom:}\quad & 2^2-(\sqrt3)^2=4-3=1\\[8pt] \text{Top:}\quad & 5(2-\sqrt3)=10-5\sqrt3 \end{aligned}\]
Answer
\(10-5\sqrt3\)
R3
Divide and write in \(a+bi\) form: \(\dfrac{3+i}{1-i}\)
\[\begin{aligned} \text{Conjugate:}\quad & 1-i\ \to\ 1+i\\[8pt] \text{Top:}\quad & (3+i)(1+i)=3+3i+i+i^2=3+4i-1=2+4i\\[8pt] \text{Bottom:}\quad & (1-i)(1+i)=1-i^2=1-(-1)=2\\[8pt] \text{Split:}\quad & \frac{2+4i}{2}=\frac22+\frac{4i}{2}=1+2i \end{aligned}\]
Answer
\(1+2i\)

Section 1.7

Combining and composing functions

Know this

  • Domain: the bottom can't be 0, and the inside of a square root can't be negative.
  • \((f\circ g)(x)=f(g(x))\): put all of \(g\) inside \(f\).
  • Domain of \(f\circ g\): \(x\) must work in \(g\), and \(g(x)\) must work in \(f\).

\((-\infty,-3)\cup(-3,3)\cup(3,\infty)\)

Section 2.4

Dividing polynomials

Know this

  • Long division loop: divide, multiply, subtract, bring down.
  • Fill missing powers with 0, like \(x^3-8=x^3+0x^2+0x-8\).
  • Remainder Theorem: \(f(c)\) equals the remainder of \(f(x)\div(x-c)\).
  • Factor Theorem: if \(f(c)=0\), then \(x-c\) is a factor.

Section 2.5

Zeros of polynomial functions

Know this

  • Possible rational zeros: \(\dfrac pq=\dfrac{\text{factors of the constant}}{\text{factors of the leading coefficient}}\).
  • Test one until you get 0, divide it out, then factor what's left.
  • Descartes: sign changes in \(f(x)\) give the positive zeros; sign changes in \(f(-x)\) give the negative zeros. Each count can also drop by 2.
  • Complex zeros come in pairs: if \(i\) is a zero, so is \(-i\).

Section 2.6

Rational functions and their graphs

Know this

  • Factor and cancel first. A canceled factor is a hole. A leftover bottom zero is a vertical asymptote.
  • Horizontal asymptote: top degree smaller means \(y=0\); same degree means leading coefficients over each other; top bigger means none.
  • Top degree exactly 1 bigger: slant asymptote. Long divide and drop the remainder.
  • Seven steps: symmetry, y-intercept, x-intercepts, vertical asymptotes, horizontal asymptote, points, draw.
16
Find the vertical asymptotes and holes: \(f(x)=\dfrac{x+5}{x^2-25}\)
\[\begin{aligned} \text{Factor:}\quad & \frac{x+5}{(x-5)(x+5)}\\[8pt] \text{Cancel:}\quad & \frac{1}{x-5},\qquad x\ne-5\\[8pt] \text{Hole:}\quad & \frac{1}{-5-5}=-\frac1{10}\Rightarrow\left(-5,-\tfrac1{10}\right)\\[8pt] \text{VA:}\quad & x-5=0\Rightarrow x=5 \end{aligned}\]
Answer
VA \(x=5\); hole at \(x=-5\), the point \(\left(-5,-\tfrac1{10}\right)\)
17
Find the vertical asymptotes, holes and horizontal asymptote: \(f(x)=\dfrac{3x^2-12}{x^2-x-2}\)
\[\begin{aligned} \text{Top:}\quad & 3(x^2-4)=3(x-2)(x+2)\\[8pt] \text{Bottom:}\quad & (x-2)(x+1)\\[8pt] \text{Cancel:}\quad & \frac{3(x+2)}{x+1},\qquad x\ne2\\[8pt] \text{Hole:}\quad & \frac{3(2+2)}{2+1}=\frac{12}{3}=4\Rightarrow(2,4)\\[8pt] \text{VA:}\quad & x+1=0\Rightarrow x=-1\\[8pt] \text{HA:}\quad & \frac{3x^2}{1x^2}\Rightarrow y=3 \end{aligned}\]
Answer
VA \(x=-1\); hole \((2,4)\); HA \(y=3\)

Section 2.7

Polynomial and rational inequalities

Know this

  • Get 0 on one side first.
  • Boundary points: zeros of the top and zeros of the bottom.
  • Test one number in each interval to get its sign.
  • \(\le\) or \(\ge\): include the top's zeros. Never include the bottom's zeros.
23

\[\begin{aligned} \text{Factor:}\quad & (x-4)(x+3)=0\Rightarrow x=-3,\ x=4\\[8pt] \text{Test:}\quad & x=-4:\ (-8)(-1)=8\ (+)\qquad x=0:\ -12\ (-)\\[4pt] & x=5:\ (1)(8)=8\ (+)\\[8pt] <0:\quad & \text{the negative interval} \end{aligned}\]
Answer
\((-3,\,4)\)
